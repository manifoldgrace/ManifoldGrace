## Practical steps
1. Choose a format and audience; obtain expressions of interest before taking money.
2. Request written venue quotes, capacity, accessibility, cancellation terms, licensing and minimum spend. Negotiate sponsorship or revenue share; avoid non-refundable commitments before demand and cash flow are established.
3. Budget venue, catering, staff including organiser pay, insurance, entertainment, ticket fees, tax, refunds and contingency. Example: 60 tickets at £35 = £2,100; venue £400, refreshments £600, other £200 = £900 illustrative surplus before omitted costs and tax. With £600 fixed costs and £25 contribution per ticket, illustrative break-even is 24 tickets. Not a forecast.
4. Confirm date, venue, accessibility, safety, licensing, insurance, refund terms and payout timing before listing on https://www.eventbrite.co.uk/organizer/overview/. Do not sell unconfirmed events.
5. Explain the intended beneficiary and net proceeds after costs; do not claim registered charity status or Gift Aid eligibility without verification. Keep accounts and publish aggregate proceeds where appropriate.
6. Review attendance, costs, feedback and actual surplus before repeating.
